Ovulation Calculator — Find Your Most Fertile Days

An Ovulation Calculator helps women track their menstrual cycle and predict their most fertile days — the window during which pregnancy is most likely to occur. By entering the first day of your last menstrual period (LMP) and your average cycle length, this tool estimates your ovulation date and the full fertile window (typically 6 days: the 5 days before ovulation plus ovulation day itself).

Whether you're trying to conceive (TTC) or simply want to understand your reproductive health better, knowing your cycle pattern is empowering.

⚕️ Medical Disclaimer: This tool provides estimates only. Consult a gynecologist or fertility specialist for medical guidance on conception planning.

How is Ovulation Date Calculated?

Ovulation Day = LMP + (Cycle Length − 14 days)

Ovulation typically occurs 14 days before the next expected period. Since cycle length varies, the calculation adjusts accordingly. For a 28-day cycle, ovulation is on Day 14. For a 32-day cycle, ovulation is on Day 18.

The fertile window includes the 5 days before ovulation plus ovulation day. Sperm can survive up to 5 days in the female reproductive tract, but an egg is viable for only 12–24 hours after release.

Frequently Asked Questions — Ovulation Calculator

What are the signs of ovulation?

Common signs include: a change in cervical mucus (becomes clear and stretchy like raw egg whites), a slight rise in basal body temperature (BBT), mild pelvic pain or cramping (mittelschmerz), breast tenderness, and an increased sex drive. An LH surge detectable by ovulation predictor kits (OPKs) is the most reliable sign.

Can I get pregnant outside the fertile window?

Pregnancy is theoretically possible only during the fertile window (the 5 days before ovulation + ovulation day), as sperm can survive up to 5 days. However, cycles can vary month-to-month, so relying solely on a calculator is not a reliable contraception method.

How accurate is this ovulation calculator?

This calculator provides estimates based on your average cycle length. It is most accurate for women with regular, predictable cycles. Stress, illness, travel, and hormonal changes can shift ovulation by several days. For greater accuracy, combine this with BBT tracking or OPK kits.

What is irregular ovulation?

Irregular ovulation (oligoovulation) or absent ovulation (anovulation) can be caused by PCOS, thyroid disorders, extreme weight changes, stress, or perimenopause. If you suspect irregular cycles, consult a gynecologist for hormonal testing.

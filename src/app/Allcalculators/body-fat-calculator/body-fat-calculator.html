Why Body Fat Percentage Matters More Than Weight

While your scale shows your total body weight, it doesn't tell the whole story. Body Fat Percentage is a far more insightful metric, revealing how much of your weight is composed of fat versus lean mass (muscle, bone, water, organs). A healthy body fat percentage is a better indicator of fitness and health than BMI, especially for individuals with more muscle mass.

This calculator uses the U.S. Navy Method, a well-established and reliable way to estimate body fat using simple body measurements. It provides a convenient and cost-effective alternative to clinical methods like DEXA scans.

How to Take Accurate Measurements

Getting accurate results starts with accurate measurements. Here’s how:

  1. Select Your Gender: The formula is different for men and women.
  2. Height & Weight: Enter your current height and weight.
  3. Neck Circumference: Place the measuring tape just below your larynx (Adam's apple) and look straight ahead. Keep the tape level.
  4. Waist Circumference: For men, measure horizontally at the level of your navel. For women, measure at the narrowest point of your abdomen, usually just above the navel.
  5. Hip Circumference (Women Only): Measure at the widest point of your hips.

For best results, use a flexible, non-stretchable measuring tape, and take each measurement two or three times to ensure accuracy. Click Calculate Body Fat to see your results.

The U.S. Navy Formula

The formulas used are derived from research by the U.S. Navy to assess the body composition of its personnel:

Men: %Fat = 495 / (1.0324 - 0.19077 * log10(Waist - Neck) + 0.15456 * log10(Height)) - 450

Women: %Fat = 495 / (1.29579 - 0.35004 * log10(Waist + Hip - Neck) + 0.22100 * log10(Height)) - 450

The results also break down your weight into Fat Mass (the weight of fat on your body) and Lean Body Mass (everything else).

Frequently Asked Questions (FAQs)

How accurate is the U.S. Navy method?

It's considered a reliable estimator, generally within 3-4% of results from more advanced methods like dual-energy X-ray absorptiometry (DEXA). However, its accuracy can be lower for individuals who are extremely lean or obese.

What's the difference between BMI and body fat percentage?

BMI is a ratio of your weight to your height, while body fat percentage measures how much of your weight is fat. Body fat percentage is a better measure of health, as it can distinguish between a muscular person and an overweight person, even if they have the same BMI.

How can I reduce my body fat percentage?

A combination of a calorie-controlled diet (eating in a slight deficit) and regular exercise, particularly strength training to build muscle, is the most effective way to reduce body fat while preserving lean mass.

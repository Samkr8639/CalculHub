What is GST and Why Was It Introduced?

GST stands for Goods and Services Tax. It is a comprehensive indirect tax applied on the manufacture, sale, and consumption of goods and services. The primary objective of GST was to eliminate the cascading effect of taxes and unify multiple indirect tax systems into one streamlined structure.

Before GST implementation, businesses had to deal with numerous taxes such as VAT, excise duty, central sales tax, octroi, luxury tax, entertainment tax, and service tax. This created complexity and increased compliance costs.

GST simplified the taxation framework by:

  • Creating One Nation, One Tax: A unified indirect taxation structure across states.
  • Reducing Tax Cascading: Eliminating “tax on tax” through Input Tax Credit.
  • Improving Transparency: Standardized invoicing and digital filing systems.
  • Boosting Compliance: Online GST portals simplified tax management.
  • Enhancing Ease of Doing Business: Businesses can operate across states more efficiently.

Today, GST is one of the most significant tax reforms in India’s economic history.

GST Formula Explained

GST calculations depend on whether tax is being added to a base amount or removed from a tax-inclusive amount.

Formula to Add GST

GST Amount = (Net Price × GST Rate) / 100
Final Price = Net Price + GST Amount

Formula to Remove GST

Net Price = Gross Price / (1 + GST Rate / 100)
GST Amount = Gross Price - Net Price
  • Net Price: Original price before GST.
  • Gross Price: Final amount including GST.
  • GST Rate: Applicable tax percentage.

These formulas ensure accurate invoice generation, tax filing, and financial reporting.

GST Tax Slabs in India

India uses multiple GST tax slabs depending on the category of goods and services.

GST Rate Applicable Goods & Services
0% Essential goods like fresh food items.
5% Mass consumption products and transport services.
12% Processed food, electronics, and certain goods.
18% Most services and standard taxable products.
28% Luxury goods, automobiles, and sin products.

The GST Council regularly reviews and updates GST rates based on economic conditions and industry requirements.

Difference Between CGST, SGST, and IGST

GST in India is divided into different components depending on the nature of the transaction.

Tax Type Used For
CGST Central GST collected by Central Government.
SGST State GST collected by State Government.
IGST Integrated GST on interstate transactions.

For transactions within the same state, GST is split equally into CGST and SGST. Interstate transactions attract IGST.

Understanding Input Tax Credit (ITC)

Input Tax Credit (ITC) is one of the most important concepts in the GST framework. ITC allows businesses to reduce their tax liability by claiming credit for GST already paid on business purchases.

For example, if a manufacturer pays GST on raw materials and later sells finished goods with GST, the GST already paid can be deducted from the final tax liability.

Reduces Tax Burden

Prevents double taxation on goods and services.

Improves Cash Flow

Businesses can offset taxes paid on purchases.

Encourages Compliance

Proper invoicing becomes essential for ITC claims.

Supports Transparency

Digital records simplify tax reconciliation.

GST Registration Rules and Threshold Limits

Businesses exceeding prescribed turnover thresholds must register under GST law.

  • Goods Businesses: GST registration is mandatory if annual turnover exceeds ₹40 Lakhs.
  • Service Businesses: GST registration is required above ₹20 Lakhs annual turnover.
  • E-Commerce Sellers: Online sellers may require GST registration regardless of turnover.
  • Interstate Suppliers: Interstate business transactions often mandate GST registration.
  • Voluntary Registration: Businesses below limits can still register voluntarily to claim ITC.

GST registration improves business credibility and enables participation in B2B supply chains.

E-Way Bills and GST Compliance

Under GST law, businesses transporting goods above prescribed invoice limits must generate an E-Way Bill.

E-Way Bills contain:

  • Supplier details
  • Recipient information
  • Invoice details
  • Transportation information
  • Goods classification data

This system helps authorities monitor goods movement and reduce tax evasion.

What is an Algebra Calculator?

An algebra calculator is a versatile online tool that helps you solve a wide range of algebraic problems, from simple linear equations to complex polynomial factoring. This calculator is designed to provide step-by-step solutions, helping you understand the underlying concepts, not just get the answer.

How to Use This Multi-Function Algebra Calculator

Our calculator features four distinct modes to handle your specific needs:

Equation Solver

  1. Select the Equation Solver tab.
  2. Enter your equation in the input box. You can solve linear equations (e.g., 2x + 5 = 15), quadratic equations (e.g., x^2 - 4x - 5 = 0), or even equations with variables on both sides.
  3. Click Solve. The calculator will provide the value of the variable and a detailed, step-by-step walkthrough of the solution process, including simplification, isolating the variable, and the final check.

Polynomial Factorer

  1. Select the Polynomial Factorer tab.
  2. Enter a polynomial expression, such as a quadratic trinomial (e.g., x^2 - 5x + 6) or a cubic expression.
  3. Click Factor. The tool will return the factored form of the polynomial (e.g., (x - 2)(x - 3)) and explain the method used, such as finding common factors, using the difference of squares, or applying the quadratic formula to find roots.

Complex Number Arithmetic

  1. Select the Complex Numbers tab.
  2. Enter two complex numbers in the format a+bi (e.g., 3+2i).
  3. Choose an operation: addition (+), subtraction (-), or multiplication (*).
  4. Click Calculate to see the resulting complex number. This is perfect for students of electrical engineering, physics, or advanced mathematics.

Inequality Grapher

  1. Select the Inequality Grapher tab.
  2. Enter an inequality involving `x` and `y` (e.g., y > 2x + 1 or x^2 + y^2 < 16).
  3. Click + Add Inequality to plot multiple inequalities on the same graph. The tool will use different colors to represent each inequality.
  4. The calculator will generate a graph showing the solution regions. The shaded area represents the set of all points that satisfy the inequality (or system of inequalities).

Frequently Asked Questions (FAQs)

What types of equations can the solver handle?

The solver is designed for linear and quadratic equations. It can find single solutions, two distinct solutions for quadratics, or report if there is no solution.

Is the step-by-step solution always available?

Yes, for the Equation Solver and Polynomial Factorer, we provide a step-by-step breakdown to help you learn the process. This is a key feature for students who want to understand the methodology.

How do I enter exponents?

Use the caret symbol (^) to denote exponents. For example, enter x^2 for x squared.

Understanding Your Metabolism: BMR and TDEE

Your body is constantly burning calories to perform life-sustaining functions like breathing, circulating blood, and repairing cells. The rate at which it does this at rest is your Basal Metabolic Rate (BMR). By understanding your BMR, you get a baseline for your daily calorie needs.

However, we don't spend all day at rest. Your Total Daily Energy Expenditure (TDEE) builds on the BMR by factoring in your daily physical activity. TDEE gives you a much more accurate estimate of the total calories you burn in a 24-hour period, making it the most important number for managing your weight.

The Science Behind the Numbers: Formulas Explained

This calculator uses the Mifflin-St Jeor Equation to estimate your BMR, which is widely regarded by dietitians and researchers as the most accurate formula for modern lifestyles.

For Men: BMR = (10 × weight in kg) + (6.25 × height in cm) - (5 × age in years) + 5

For Women: BMR = (10 × weight in kg) + (6.25 × height in cm) - (5 × age in years) - 161

Your TDEE is then calculated by multiplying your BMR by an activity multiplier:

  • TDEE = BMR × Activity Multiplier
  • Multipliers range from 1.2 (sedentary) to 1.9 (extra active).

How to Use Your TDEE for Weight Management

  • To Lose Weight: You must consume fewer calories than your TDEE. A deficit of 500 calories per day will lead to approximately 0.5 kg (1 lb) of weight loss per week.
  • To Maintain Weight: Consume a number of calories equal to your TDEE.
  • To Gain Weight: You must consume more calories than your TDEE. A surplus of 500 calories per day will lead to approximately 0.5 kg (1 lb) of weight gain per week, which is ideal for muscle building when combined with strength training.

Frequently Asked Questions (FAQs)

What is the difference between BMR and TDEE?

BMR is the energy your body burns to survive while at complete rest (like when you're asleep). TDEE is the total energy you burn all day, which includes your BMR plus calories burned from physical activity, digestion, and other daily movements.

How accurate is this calculator?

The Mifflin-St Jeor equation provides a very accurate estimation for most people. However, factors like body composition (muscle vs. fat), genetics, and health conditions can cause individual variations. Use this as a starting point and adjust based on your real-world results.

Should I eat my BMR or TDEE?

You should use your TDEE as the guide for your daily calorie intake, not your BMR. Eating only your BMR is not sustainable and would be considered a starvation diet if you are active.
